TITLE (sentence case, no abbreviations):
Rarity and local order: two complementary label-free channels for anomaly detection on lattices of embeddings

ABSTRACT (198 words, limit 200):
Anomaly scores on lattices of embeddings, such as image patches, usually measure how rare the embedding of a site is. We show on simulated data that rarity is one of two complementary label-free channels. Channel A is the rarity aggregated with weights given by the similarity of neighboring embeddings; it preserved scattered and bar-shaped anomalies that a fixed 3x3 mean destroyed (lower-tail average precision 0.32 against 0.11 on ten independent test scenarios), and a contrast-survival model explains the loss. Channel B is a directional form of the local disorder of the MyUncle lattice model, the minimum of the disorder over the four quadrants of a site. It detects arrangement anomalies, the same embeddings in the wrong places: there the rarity stayed at chance (average precision 0.06) and channel B reached 0.86. A label-free tail index chooses the channel per cohort; on fifteen independent test scenarios this raised the lower-tail average precision from 0.20 to 0.36 (paired 95% interval of the gain +0.10 to +0.25) without loss on the scenarios with feature anomalies only. Order gates and an evolutionary configuration search did not help, and cohorts with both kinds of anomaly need both maps. Code and results are open.

KEYWORDS:
anomaly detection; embedding lattices; spatial arrangement; orientational order; similarity-weighted aggregation; label-free fusion

HIGHLIGHTS (3-5, <=85 characters each):
- Rarity scores are blind to anomalies of arrangement; local order detects them
- Directional local disorder ignores domain walls and marks disorganized regions
- A label-free tail index chooses the channel per cohort, with no loss elsewhere
- Similarity-weighted aggregation keeps anomalies that a fixed window destroys
- Order gates and evolutionary rule search did not help; reported as negatives
